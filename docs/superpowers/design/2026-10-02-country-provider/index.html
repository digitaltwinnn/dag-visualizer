Country and provider — the same card twice

Open a country, then a provider inside it, and the rail shows two cards with the same shape and nearly the same content: a node count, the same cut by network, then a row or two. The provider card's table is usually a smaller copy of the country's. Four ways to make each card say something the other does not.

Each option shows the two cards as they stack in the rail (country above, provider below). Germany, 62 nodes; Hetzner in Falkenstein, 31 of them. The splits are illustrative. Today's cards are option A.

Recommend B. Each card answers the question its own level raises and hands off to the next: the country says who hosts here (its providers), the provider says whose nodes these are (the networks). Read down the pile it is place, then host, then network, with no table repeated. It needs no new element, only a different cut on the country card. C is the stronger picture, since the provider's share of the country is the one figure neither card shows today, and it can be added to B later.
