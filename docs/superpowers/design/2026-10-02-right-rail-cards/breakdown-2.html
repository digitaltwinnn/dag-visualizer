Breakdown, round two — D, with the legend as a table

You liked the unit squares and disliked the dot legend. These four keep "every node a square" and replace the legend with aligned columns: no dots, names and counts in a table, the colour key carried by a thin rule or by the squares themselves. Each is drawn for a small network (live figures) and for the largest one as it will be after the reduction to about 30 nodes, so a square is always exactly one node and nothing is scaled.

Round one is here; the skeleton page is here. Hover a square for its part. D4's cross-figures are illustrative: the data to compute them exists per node, the split shown is not measured.

Recommend D2. It is one table, so every name and every count aligns down the card, and the squares in each row are both the bar and the colour key, which is what removes the legend altogether. A row holds about fifteen squares before it wraps, so at 30 nodes the largest part takes a second line and nothing else does. D4 is the one that adds information rather than rearranging it, and is worth it if "which nodes are ready" by composition is a question you want the card to answer.
