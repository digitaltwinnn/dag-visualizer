2 · The skeleton

Six slots in one order. A card uses the slots it has content for and skips the rest, so a short card is the same card with fewer slots, not a different design.

  1. 1 Head Eyebrow, glyph, title, and ONE aside that is either a qualifier chip (ticker, role, country code, city) or a state pill (ready, live / pinned). Names in sans, machine ids and ordinals in mono. Relations and ages leave the aside.
  2. 2 Lead One sentence in dim ink, two lines at most, saying what this is in relation to its parent. The metagraph's subtitle and description merge here, clamped with "more". Relations and ages land here ("Anchored to 6,989,808 · 35s ago").
  3. 3 Breakdown A caps section label with its total on the right, then the breakdown. The dossier keeps its stacked bars; a per-network cut uses the bar rows. The label line is where the card's one headline figure lives.
  4. 4 Facts Label left, value right, regular weight. One dash for every empty value, with the reason on hover. Sub-notes become their own fact ("Size 50 KB") or join the value line.
  5. 5 Doors Every way out of the card is one full-bleed row: label, target, glyph. Site, Decompress, Snapshot records.
  6. 6 Foot + pager Identifiers on the plate (as built today), the pager always last.
